**Quick answer:** Unplug it, empty the basin and let every part air-dry daily. Once a week, soak the removable cloud and basin in equal parts white vinegar and water for 20–30 minutes, brush the drip holes, wipe the mist plate gently, then rinse thoroughly. Never submerge the base, never mix cleaners, and don’t use boiling water.

That’s the whole routine. Below is why it matters, plus step-by-step instructions and fixes for the two problems people search for most: clogged rain holes and pink film.

> **Always check your manual first.** Designs vary. If your manufacturer’s instructions differ from this guide, follow theirs.

## Why Open-Drip Cloud Humidifiers Need Regular Cleaning

Rain cloud humidifiers are small tabletop water features. Water sits in an open basin, gets pumped through a tube and dripped through tiny holes — often for hours, under a warm LED. That combination creates two problems:

1. **Minerals.** Tap water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium. As water evaporates and mists, those minerals stay behind as white, chalky scale. In a rain cloud unit, the first place they build up is the drip holes, which are small enough to clog quickly.
2. **Microbial growth.** Standing water and damp plastic are where bacteria, film and mold grow. Ultrasonic mist can carry some of what’s in the water into the air, which is exactly why general humidifier guidance stresses changing the water daily and keeping tanks clean.

The good news: a small device means a small job. You’re cleaning a cup and a quarter of water, not a five-liter tank.

## The 60-Second Daily Habit (Empty, Rinse, Air-Dry)

Do this every day you use the unit.

1. **Unplug it.** Always disconnect power before handling water.
2. **Lift off the cloud** and set it aside.
3. **Empty the basin** into a sink. Don’t tip water over the base’s buttons or power port.
4. **Rinse** the basin and the underside of the cloud with clean water.
5. **Wipe dry** with a soft, lint-free cloth.
6. **Leave it open** — cloud off, basin uncovered — so it dries completely before the next fill.
7. **Refill with fresh water** next time you use it. Don’t top up old water.

That last point is the habit that prevents most problems. Topping up yesterday’s water keeps the cycle going; fresh water each time breaks it.

## Weekly Deep Clean Using White Vinegar (Step-by-Step)

White vinegar is mildly acidic. It dissolves mineral scale and helps clean away film without the harshness of stronger chemicals.

**You’ll need:** plain white vinegar, water, a soft brush (an old soft toothbrush or the brush supplied with your unit), cotton swabs, and a soft cloth.

1. **Unplug and empty** the unit.
2. **Mix equal parts white vinegar and water.** For heavy scale, you can use undiluted vinegar on the removable parts.
3. **Soak the removable parts.** Submerge the cloud top and any removable basin or tray for 20–30 minutes. If the basin is built into the base, fill it with the mix up to the normal water line only, keeping liquid away from the power port and buttons.
4. **Brush gently.** Scrub the inside of the cloud reservoir, the drip holes and the basin with the soft brush.
5. **Clean the mist plate.** Dip a cotton swab in the vinegar mix and gently wipe the small round ceramic disc at the bottom of the basin. Don’t press hard, and never scrape it with anything metal — it’s delicate.
6. **Flush the pump path.** Some units let you run plain water through the pump after cleaning. If yours does, refill with clean water, run the rain for a minute, then empty. Never run the unit with vinegar in it.
7. **Rinse thoroughly.** Rinse every part two or three times until there’s no vinegar smell.
8. **Air-dry completely** before reassembling.

**Optional disinfecting step:** Some manufacturers suggest 3% hydrogen peroxide for small humidifiers. Use it only if your manual allows it, on its own (never combined with vinegar or anything else), and rinse thoroughly afterward.

## How to Clear Clogged Rain Holes Without Scratching Plastic

If the rain has thinned to a few drops or one sad drip, the holes are probably partly blocked with scale or film.

1. **Soak first.** Give the cloud top a 30-minute vinegar soak. Most mineral clogs soften or dissolve.
2. **Brush from both sides.** Use a soft brush or a soft interdental brush on the inside of the cloud reservoir and on the underside where the drops form.
3. **Flush.** Hold the cloud under a running tap so water flows through the holes.
4. **For stubborn holes:** use a plastic flosser pick or a soft bristle, gently. Avoid metal pins and needles; they can scratch or widen the holes, which makes drops uneven and splashy.
5. **Test.** Reassemble with clean water and run the rain. Even, scattered drops mean you’re done.

If the holes clear but the rain still doesn’t flow, the problem may be an air lock or a low water level instead. Our guide on how to [troubleshoot clogged drip holes](/guides/rain-cloud-humidifier-not-dripping-fix/) walks through every other cause.

## Dealing With Pink Film or Cloudy Buildup Safely

**Pink or orange film** on damp surfaces is common in bathrooms and humidifiers alike. It’s usually caused by a widespread airborne bacterium that thrives on moist surfaces with a little residue.

**Cloudy white buildup** is usually mineral scale from tap water.

**Black or green spots or a musty smell** suggest mold.

What to do:

1. **Stop using it** until it’s clean.
2. **Do the full weekly vinegar clean** above, with extra time on the affected areas.
3. **Scrub gently** with a soft brush, including corners and the underside of the cloud.
4. **Rinse well and dry completely** — dryness is what stops regrowth.
5. **Tighten up your daily habit.** Recurring pink film nearly always means water is sitting too long.

If mold keeps returning in places you can’t reach, or the plastic is stained deep inside the pump path, it’s time to replace the device rather than keep running it.

## What Never to Use (Dish Soap, Bleach, Boiling Water)

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| **Lots of dish soap** | Residue is hard to rinse out of tiny holes and can foam in the basin. If you use a drop, rinse very thoroughly. |
| **Bleach mixed with vinegar** | Never mix bleach with vinegar or other acids — the combination releases toxic chlorine gas. |
| **Bleach on its own** | Only if your manual specifically allows it, heavily diluted, and rinsed thoroughly. |
| **Boiling water** | Can warp thin plastic and damage seals. |
| **Dishwasher** | Heat warps parts; the base contains electronics. |
| **Abrasive pads or powders** | Scratch plastic and the mist plate, creating places for film to grow. |
| **Submerging the base** | The pump, mist plate driver, LED and USB port are electrical. Wipe the outside only. |
| **Essential oils as a “cleaner”** | They coat the mist plate and can degrade plastics. |

## Make Cleaning Easier Next Time

- **Use distilled or filtered water.** Fewer minerals means less scale and fewer clogged holes. Our [distilled water vs. tap water guide](/guides/distilled-water-and-essential-oils/) explains the trade-offs.
- **Empty it before storage** and store it fully dry, with the cloud off.
- **Keep it out of direct sunlight,** which encourages algae in clear basins.

If you’re choosing a unit, look for a basin you can actually reach into and a cloud that lifts off in one piece. The [Mistlo Cloud Lamp](/) was designed that way, with a smooth basin and a soft drip-hole brush in the box — but the routine above works for any rain cloud humidifier.
