# About Mistlo: How This AI Portfolio Store Was Built

**Portfolio experiment — no products are sold.** Mistlo doesn’t exist. There is no warehouse, no checkout and no order form. When you press **Buy**, you see a short reveal instead of a payment page. Nothing is charged, and no personal data is collected — no names, emails, addresses or card details, and no tracking cookies.

## Why build a store for a product that doesn’t exist?

I’m Gavin. I wanted to answer a practical question with real numbers instead of opinions:

**How far can a small team of AI agents take a brand-new website — from zero search presence to people genuinely deciding to buy?**

Most claims about AI and marketing are screenshots and promises. This project is a live test. It started with a blank domain, no backlinks and no ad budget. Everything you see — the research, the product, the copy, the guides, the design and the code — was produced by AI agents working in defined roles, with me setting the goal and the rules.

The product is a rain cloud humidifier and night light because it’s a real, searched-for category: people genuinely want to know whether these things work, how loud they are and how to clean them. That gave the experiment honest search demand to earn.

## How the work was split

Four roles, one folder each, nobody editing anyone else’s work. It’s the same structure I’d use on a client project.

### 1. Research — Gemini

Gemini handled everything search-related: checking that the original product name didn’t collide with existing brands (it did, so we renamed it Mistlo), mapping 42 keywords by intent and competition, planning the site structure, writing every title tag and meta description, and producing the technical SEO checklist — structured data, canonicals, sitemap and Core Web Vitals targets.

### 2. Build — ChatGPT (Codex)

Codex built the site: a fast static site with no heavy frameworks, the Buy-to-reveal interaction, structured data, sitemap, privacy-friendly analytics events and the deploy pipeline. All brand details live in one config file, so the whole store could be renamed in a minute.

### 3. Write — Claude

A Claude writing agent produced the product page, the FAQ, this page and seven long-form guides. The guides were written to be genuinely useful about rain cloud humidifiers in general — accurate cleaning steps, honest limits, real safety cautions — because that’s what deserves to rank, and because a searcher should leave better informed whether or not they ever click Buy.

### 4. Manage — Claude

A second Claude agent acted as project manager: writing the brief and the hard rules, sequencing the work, integrating each agent’s output, and reviewing everything against the rules before launch.

## The rules every agent followed

Persuasion is easy if you’re willing to lie. The point was to see how far it goes without that. Every agent worked under the same non-negotiable rules:

- **No fake social proof.** No invented reviews, testimonials, star ratings or “as seen in” logos — and no review markup in the structured data.
- **No health or medical claims.** The product is described as a desk and bedside object, not a wellness device.
- **No real brands named** as competitors or endorsers.
- **White-hat SEO only.** Original, useful content, accurate metadata, internal links and speed — no keyword stuffing, cloaking, doorway pages or link schemes. Search engines and visitors see exactly the same pages.
- **No data collection.** No forms, no cookies, no personal information.
- **Free tools only.** Free hosting, free analytics, free search tools.

So everything that persuades on this site is design, specificity and honesty: clear specs, an “honest fit” section that tells people when *not* to buy, and guides that admit what these devices can’t do.

## What’s measured, and why

The experiment tracks a simple funnel, using only privacy-friendly tools.

| Stage | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Search impressions & clicks** | How often the site appears in Google results, for which queries, and how often people click | Google Search Console |
| **Visitors** | How many people arrive, and from where | GoatCounter (cookieless) |
| **Product views** | How many reach the product page | GoatCounter event |
| **Buy clicks** | How many were persuaded enough to press Buy | GoatCounter event |
| **Reveal, share and portfolio clicks** | How people react to the reveal | GoatCounter events |

The headline result is one sentence: **X search impressions → Y visitors → Z clicked Buy.** That conversion rate is the honest measure of whether AI-researched, AI-written, AI-built content can earn attention *and* trust — without fake reviews or dark patterns.

GoatCounter counts visits without cookies or personal data, so there’s no consent banner because there’s nothing to consent to.


## What this means for your project

The same workflow — specialist agents for research, build and writing, coordinated by a manager agent under clear rules — works for real businesses:

- **Launching a product or service** and needing a credible site, search strategy and content quickly.
- **Building an SEO content library** that answers real customer questions accurately.
- **Testing positioning and messaging** before spending on ads.

The difference between a gimmick and a useful AI workflow is the brief, the guardrails and the review. That’s the part I bring.
